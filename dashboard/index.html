Wind tunnel: test market rules before you deploy them.

Weight the auction by reputation: lowest-price-wins cut the price paid from 0.48 to 0.12 of the reward, but task success fell from 81% to 57%. A 10% fee recycled into demand cut the largest family from 94% to 75% of agents, with no detectable change in price, task success or survivors.

Simulated, not this live run: end-of-run means over 10 seeds of 2,000 rounds each, random mutation, one rule changed at a time.

The detail: each rule set runs on the same 10 seeds (make windtunnel). Each cell is the mean over seeds; underneath, the change from the baseline. Bright changes held up in a paired permutation test (p < 0.01); grey ones did not.

Outcomes of six market rule sets, mean over 10 seeds, and the change from the baseline

This shared run is read-only. To try a rule set, run DARWIN locally (make demo) and press Try it.

If you’re designing an agent market

  1. Weight the auction by reputation. Lowest-price-wins cut what buyers paid from 0.48 to 0.12 of the reward, and task success fell from 81% to 57%: the cheapest bidder was usually the sloppiest.
  2. A fee recycled into demand curbs concentration cheaply. A 10% fee back to the task pool cut the largest family’s share from 94% to 75% with no detectable change in price, quality or survivors. Founding families left: 1.3 to 2.8, a tentative result (p = 0.016).
  3. Rent is a blunter tool. Doubling upkeep also broke up the leading family, but cost 7 living agents (23 to 16) and 31 more extinctions.
  4. Find what binds before you buy capacity. Doubling solver capacity changed nothing in any seed: three resold jobs a round never ran out. The guard leaked 0 credits and supply was conserved in all 60 runs.

Caveats: one simulator, the knobs it has, 10 seeds, one knob at a time. Absolute numbers are the sim’s, not a forecast. The full tables, every p-value and the null results: results/windtunnel.md.
